# Curriculum coverage vs. the career-path guide

Audit of the SkillMap curriculum against `marketing-career-paths-and-skills-guide.md` —
its 16 major career paths (§3.1–3.16) and its cross-cutting core capabilities (§2).

Before: 47 skills / 48 courses. After: **53 skills / 54 courses / 232 lessons.**

## What was added

Six skills, each with a five-lesson course. Demand scores calibrated against the existing 60–98
spread — operational and governance disciplines are genuinely in demand but not top-tier, so none
were given a score that would outrank AI Marketing or Performance Marketing.

| Skill | Territory | Demand | Course | Fills |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Marketing Operations & RevOps | Lifecycle & Automation | 82 | Marketing Operations & RevOps | §3.11 |
| Campaign & Project Management | Strategy & Brand | 76 | Campaign & Project Management | §2 project management |
| Marketing Finance & Budgeting | Analytics & Data | 74 | Marketing Finance & Budgeting | §2 commercial literacy |
| Privacy, Ethics & Governance | Analytics & Data | 71 | Privacy, Ethics & Governance | §2 ethics/privacy/governance |
| Events & Field Marketing | Creative & Web | 65 | Events & Field Marketing | §3.14 |
| Shopper & Channel Marketing | Strategy & Brand | 62 | Shopper & Channel Marketing | §3.15 |

No new skill *category* was needed — all six fit the existing seven territories.

Lesson content is grounded in what the guide actually says about each path (skills to build,
typical work, portfolio evidence), and follows the existing house style: a few short paragraphs
ending in a concrete `Action:` line.

## What was deliberately not added

- **A general "Digital Marketing" course** — see the §3.5 note. It would restate the channel courses.
- **Sector variants** (healthcare, B2B SaaS, non-profit marketing). The guide treats these as
  contexts you apply the paths within, not as separate paths.
- **Career-development content** (§4 career levels, §6 certifications, §7 the 12-month plan,
  §8 choosing a path, §11 first choices by profile). These are guidance about navigating a career,
  not marketing skills to learn. If the product ever wants a "plan your path" surface, this is the
  material for it — it is a product feature, not a course.
- **Design and copy craft as separate disciplines** beyond what Visual Design & Canva and
  Copywriting already cover; the guide is explicit that marketers need judgment here, not
  production skill.
